Reusable micro-widget

Make the navigation itself carry reading progress.

This fixture captures a compact long-form pattern inspired by section-aware progress demos: instead of a detached top bar, the reader sees a sticky local rail that grows across the sections currently in view.

Intent

Local progress feels more grounded than a global meter.

When the page already has a named section list, progress becomes easier to interpret if it travels through that same list. Readers do not have to map "62%" back to meaning. They can see which named chunks are live right now.
Best fit Editorial docs, product case studies, research reports, and launch explainers.
Not a fit Short landing pages or feeds where a sticky left rail would be heavier than the content.
Structure

Short labels, one rail, and generous spacing do most of the work.

The pattern works because the labels stay brief. The left side is not a second article; it is a persistent orientation aid. The active marker earns attention through contrast and movement, not through loud color everywhere.
If a reader can understand "where am I?" and "what is next?" without parsing a separate widget, the progress treatment is doing its job.
Motion

Expanding the marker across the visible span reads better than a single dot.

Long sections often overlap in the viewport. A dot that snaps between anchors can feel jittery, especially on tall screens. A span communicates that the reader is between states and preserves a calmer rhythm while scrolling.
Entry easing Use a decisive ease-out on the span movement so the marker settles quickly after each scroll gesture.
Visible range The active fill can stretch from the first visible section to the last visible section, which makes overlapping content feel intentional.
Edge cases

Overlapping sections, mobile collapse, and very short content need explicit handling.

The rail should still make sense when two sections are partially visible, when a section is too short to dominate the viewport, or when the layout collapses to one column on mobile. On small screens, the same section list can become a regular in-flow summary instead of a sticky sidebar.
Reuse

Use it as an orientation device, not as spectacle.

This pattern is strongest when the content itself is dense enough to warrant help. It does not need oversized animation, glow effects, or a second progress bar elsewhere on the page.
