# Reel Scripting — Short-Form Script Structure

Reels (Instagram, YouTube Shorts) follow different rules from long-form content. The
margin for error is near-zero — you have 3 seconds to earn the next 3, and then the
next 30, and then the full duration. Every sentence must justify its existence.

---

## The Three-Part Reel Structure

Every successful reel has exactly three sections. There is no fourth.

```
HOOK     (0–3 seconds)   → Earn their attention
BODY     (4s to end-3s)  → Deliver the value
CTA      (last 2–3s)     → Give them one action
```

Never merge these. Never skip the hook and go straight to value. Never add a second CTA.

---

## Section 1: The Hook (0–3 Seconds)

The hook is the only part of the reel that determines whether anyone watches the rest.
A perfect body and CTA mean nothing if the hook fails.

**The hook's one job:** Create an open loop. Give the viewer a question their brain
cannot leave unanswered. They will keep watching to close the loop.

### Hook Types

**Question Hook**
Trigger an internal question the viewer must answer.
> "क्या तुम्हें पता है कि ChatGPT अब सांस भी ले सकता है?"
> "What would you do if your savings account got hacked — right now?"

Rule: The question must feel personal. "Have you ever failed?" is too generic.
"Have you ever typed your card details into a site and immediately regretted it?" is specific.

**Conflict/Stakes Hook**
Introduce a contradiction or threat immediately.
> "Recently, HDFC Bank, Instagram, fashion brands — all launched fake campaigns. And it was
> all to save you."
> "IPL isn't just a cricket league. It's the most expensive 2 months of your financial life."

**Celebrity/Credibility Hook**
Open with a known figure saying something provocative. Borrow their credibility to earn
the viewer's trust in the first second.
> [Opens on Gary Vee clip] "This technology can change a ton of people's lives..."
> [Opens on Sandeep Maheshwari] "Let's take a simple use case..."

Rule: The celebrity must be saying something that directly sets up YOUR topic. Don't use
a famous face just as decoration.

**Demo Hook**
Show the result or outcome before explaining how it works. Reverse the logical order
on purpose.
> [Shows AI avatar speaking with breathing sounds] "I am not real."
> [Shows finished design] "This took me 4 minutes. Here's how."

The viewer has to stay to find out how the result was achieved.

**Caption Hook (Text on Screen)**
Sometimes the visual or text caption does the hook's job with no narration needed.
Use this when your demo or footage is compelling enough to speak for itself.
> Text: "ChatGPT voices can now breathe" over a screen recording of exactly that.
> Text: "I went from 50K to 100K followers in 30 days" over a casual talking-head clip.

This is the most underused hook type. A strong, incomplete caption above raw footage
outperforms a scripted verbal hook 40% of the time.

### Hook Mistakes to Avoid

| Mistake | Example | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Starting with "So today I want to talk about..." | "So today I want to talk about AI" | Cut to the first interesting sentence |
| Generic question | "Have you ever wondered about technology?" | Make it specific and personal |
| Slow visual | Talking head for 5+ seconds with no movement | Cut to B-roll, graphic, or demo by second 2 |
| Revealing the answer in the hook | "AI can breathe now — here's how to enable it" | Withhold the answer. Show the result, not the method |
| Apologizing or warming up | "I know this might sound weird but..." | Remove entirely. Start with the weird thing. |

## Section 2: The Body

The body delivers the value you promised in the hook. It must be:
- **Dense** — no wasted sentences
- **Fast** — visual change every 2 seconds (cut, text overlay, B-roll change)
- **Escalating** — each beat must be slightly more interesting than the last

### Sentence Cadence Rule
No sentence in the body should take more than 3–4 seconds to say. If it does, cut it into
two sentences. Reel viewers process information in bursts, not paragraphs.

### Visual Cadence Rule
Every 2 seconds, something on screen must change:
- Cut to a different angle
- New text overlay appears
- B-roll footage appears
- Graphics change
- Reaction cut

If the screen is static for more than 2 seconds in a reel, retention drops sharply.

### Value Density Rule
Every sentence must either:
- Give new information
- Raise the stakes
- Answer part of the hook's question (but not fully — save that for the end)
- Create a new micro-question

If a sentence does none of these four things, cut it.

### The Conflict Engine
Great reel bodies follow a rhythm of: **reveal → conflict → reveal → conflict.**
Each new piece of information introduces a new problem or contradiction. This is what
creates the dopamine loop that keeps viewers watching.

Example structure for a 60-second reel:
```
[Hook: question raised]
[Beat 1: partial answer — but there's a problem]
[Beat 2: the problem is bigger than expected]
[Beat 3: there's actually a solution — but it's surprising]
[Beat 4: show the solution / proof]
[CTA]
```

### Using Celebrities and Proof Points in the Body
Social proof mid-body keeps credibility high. Insert:
- Quick reaction cuts of a known figure responding to something
- Screen recordings showing real results (tool UI, analytics)
- News article headlines as visual B-roll
- Before/after comparisons

These are not decorations — they are credibility beats. Place one every 15–20 seconds
in longer reels.

### Transitions Between Beats
Use transitions to maintain flow. Transition types for reels:
- **Lens flare** (seamless, high-production feel)
- **Cut on action** (cut exactly when a movement completes)
- **Text card** (brief title card that names the next section)

Avoid pan/swipe transitions — they signal that the content changed, which breaks immersion.

## Section 3: The CTA (Call to Action)

The CTA is the last 2–3 seconds. One action. Not two, not three. One.

**The four CTA types:**

| Type | When to use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Follow** | Audience is new, this was a pull reel | "For more content on [topic], follow [handle]" |
| **Save** | Content is reference material (checklist, how-to) | "Save this for next time you need to [do X]" |
| **Comment** | You want engagement to boost the algorithm | "Tell me in comments: [specific question]" |
| **Share** | Content is emotionally triggering or very useful | "Send this to someone who needs to see this" |

**Rules:**
- Say the CTA naturally, as part of the script — not as an afterthought
- Never say "like, share, and subscribe" in a reel — too long, feels desperate
- The CTA should logically follow from the topic. If you made a video about a tool,
  "follow me for more tool discoveries" makes sense. "Like this video" does not.

## Reel Script Templates

### Template 1: Single Fact Reel (30–45 sec)
```
[Hook — 1 sentence: shocking fact or question]
[Context — 1–2 sentences: why this matters]
[Reveal — 2–3 sentences: the core value / answer]
[Proof — 1 sentence: source, demo, or example]
[CTA — 1 sentence]
```

### Template 2: Tutorial/Tool Reel (45–75 sec)
```
[Hook — problem statement or demo of result]
[Stakes — why this problem matters / costs you]
[Solution reveal — name the tool or method]
[Step 1 → visual]
[Step 2 → visual]
[Step 3 → visual]
[Outcome — show what it produces]
[CTA — save this / follow for more]
```

### Template 3: Conflict/Controversy Reel (60–90 sec)
```
[Hook — conflict introduced immediately]
[Side A — the commonly believed version]
[Evidence — data or example supporting Side A]
[Conflict beat — "but here's what they're not telling you"]
[Side B — the real or hidden version]
[Evidence — proof of Side B]
[Takeaway — one-sentence verdict]
[CTA — comment your take / share with someone]
```

### Template 4: Storytelling Reel (Blend with Storyteller Skill)
When Storyteller skill is active, use this template:
```
[Scene Hook — P.A.S.T. opening: Place + Action]
[Stakes — what is at risk]
[Adversity peak — Speech + Thoughts from P.A.S.T.]
[Resolution — punchy, 1–2 sentences]
[Takeaway — single lesson sentence from CART]
[CTA]
```

## Reel Length Guide

| Length | Best for | Difficulty |
|---|---|---|
| 15–30 sec | Single fact, meme-format, caption-driven | Easy |
| 30–60 sec | Tutorial, tool demo, quick explanation | Medium |
| 60–90 sec | Controversy, conflict, storytelling | Hard |
| 90 sec+ | Mini-documentary format | Very hard |

**Rule:** Every second beyond 60 requires exponentially better scripting. If you can't
script a 60-second reel that holds attention, do not attempt 90 seconds. Master 30-second
precision first, then expand.

---

## The Subtitle Rule

Caption placement affects retention.
- Subtitles at the bottom third = easy to ignore
- Subtitles in the center or upper third = hard to ignore
- Max 2–3 words per subtitle card (not full sentences)
- No karaoke (word-by-word highlight) — it's distracting in 2025+ trend cycles
- Use bold, high-contrast font on a semi-transparent background

## Creator vs Curator Mode

You do not always need to create original content from scratch.

**Creator mode:** You write the script, appear on camera, build the concept yourself.
High effort. Builds the strongest personal brand.

**Curator mode:** You take an existing idea (a tweet, a quote, a thread, a clip) and
package it in a more compelling, visual, well-produced way. Lower effort. Still builds
brand if you add your own context and credit the source.

Curator reel example: Find a powerful quote on Twitter. Record yourself reading it into
your iPhone mic. Layer movie footage over it. Add music. Change scene every 1 second.
Credit the original author in the caption. This is not plagiarism — it is curation.

Both modes are valid. Beginners should do 2 curator reels for every 1 creator reel while
building scripting confidence.
