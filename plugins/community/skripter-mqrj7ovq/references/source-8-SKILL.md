# Instagram Hook Writer

## What this skill does

Generates hooks for short-form video (Instagram Reels, TikTok, YouTube Shorts) using a battle-tested 3-step psychological formula, not a generic list of "viral hook templates." The formula works because it manufactures a **curiosity loop**: it gets the viewer to lean in, stuns them to a stop, then snaps them in a new direction they can't look away from.

Every hook this skill produces should make the viewer feel like they slammed on the brakes while scrolling at highway speed — and now they *have* to know what comes next.

## Before writing hooks: get the inputs

Don't ask the user to fill out a form. Pull what you can from context (topic, niche, draft script, target audience) and proceed. Only ask if something critical and non-inferable is missing — at most one quick question. Useful things to know, in priority order:

1. **Topic/niche** — what's the video actually about?
2. **The single most surprising/counterintuitive fact, benefit, or pain point** in the content. This is the fuel for the Snapback — without a genuinely compelling payoff, the formula produces a hollow hook. If the user hasn't given you one, ask for it or propose a few candidates pulled from what they've shared and let them pick.
3. **Format** — is this entertainment/educational content, or B2B/lead-gen (real estate, coaching, services, etc.)? This changes tone but not the underlying structure.
4. **Audience** — who should self-select IN to this video?

If the user gives you a full script or detailed topic, just extract the inputs yourself and proceed — don't interrogate them.

## The core formula: Context Lean → Scroll-Stop Interjection → Contrarian Snapback

This is the foundation. Apply it to every hook request before layering on the supporting tactics below. Read `references/techniques.md` for the full breakdown with worked examples (the Vegas Sphere video, the real estate mortgage hook, etc.) — read it before writing hooks for anything beyond the simplest one-liner, since the worked examples make the pattern much easier to apply correctly.

### Step 1 — Context Lean (1-2 lines)
Do two things simultaneously:
- **Topic clarity**: state plainly what this is about, so the right viewer self-selects in (and the wrong viewer bounces — that's fine, don't bait-and-switch).
- **Get them leaning in**, using one of:
  - Establish common ground ("if you've ever struggled with X...")
  - Reference a benefit or pain point they already have
  - Use a metaphor to simplify something complex
  - State something surprising enough to make them want to know more

### Step 2 — Scroll-Stop Interjection (1 short line)
A single line that acts like a stun gun, stopping the lean-in cold. The mechanism: use a contrasting word — **but, however, yet, although, therefore, on the other hand** — to set up the reversal. This line's only job is to make the reversal in Step 3 land. It is a setup line, not the payoff.

### Step 3 — Contrarian Snapback (the haymaker)
A line that goes in the **opposite direction** of the Step 1 lean, while staying on-topic. The bigger the shock relative to the setup, the bigger the snap, the stronger the hook. This is where the real, compelling payoff goes — the genuinely surprising fact, the real benefit, the actual mistake. Never snap back to something underwhelming; a weak payoff after a strong setup is worse than no hook at all.

**Template shape:**
```
[Topic + something that makes you lean in]. But/however/yet [stun line]. [Surprising reversal that's actually compelling].
```

**Worked pattern (entertainment):**
> The tech in the Vegas Sphere is insane — biggest screen ever built, 20x bigger than an IMAX. But get this: the screen is actually the least impressive part of the whole thing. Because the most impressive part is the audio.

**Worked pattern (B2B / lead-gen, e.g. real estate, finance, coaching):**
> There are three massive mistakes people make with their mortgage. The average person overpays by $122,000 — and most assume it's because of high interest rates. But it has nothing to do with that. The real waste is coming from [X].

The formula works in any niche because it's psychology, not topic-dependent phrasing — apply the *structure*, not literal templates.

## Supporting tactics (apply on top of the core formula)

After drafting the 3-step core, strengthen the hook with whichever of these fit the format. Full detail and examples in `references/techniques.md`.

1. **Visual hook pairing** — for video, always also propose: (a) 3-5 words of bold on-screen text that compresses the Context Lean visually, and (b) a note on what visual/motion should accompany it. Text + motion + spoken hook together outperform spoken word alone by a wide margin. Don't skip this even if the user only asked for "the words" — flag it as a bonus.
2. **Benefit-first framing** — lead with the payoff/pain-point the viewer cares about, then introduce the topic as the solution, rather than assuming prior interest in the subject itself. ("If you want better sleep, you need to be taking X" beats "X is one of the 21 core minerals.")
3. **Cultural anchoring ("cult hopping")** — when the topic is complex or niche, wrap it in a known reference (a celebrity, brand, or cultural moment) as a comparison point, to borrow familiarity and credibility. Use sparingly and only when it's a natural fit — forced celebrity name-drops read as try-hard.
4. **Speed-to-value** — wherever possible, get a genuine nugget of value into the hook itself, or the line immediately after it, rather than saving all payoff for later. Don't write a hook that's pure tease with zero substance.
5. **Staccato pacing** — keep hook sentences short and punchy. Maximize clarity and density of meaning per word up front; longer, flowing sentences can come later in the video, never in the hook.

## Output format

For each hook request, deliver:

1. **The hook itself**, broken into its three labeled parts (Context Lean / Scroll-Stop / Snapback) so the user can see the mechanics, followed by the hook written as it would actually be spoken/captioned (no labels, ready to use).
2. **A suggested on-screen text overlay** (3-5 words).
3. **A one-line motion/visual note** (what should be happening on screen during the hook).
4. **3 variants minimum** when the user hasn't specified otherwise — vary the angle (different benefit/pain point, different Snapback) rather than just rewording the same idea, so the user has real options.

Keep the tone matched to the user's niche — playful for entertainment/lifestyle, sharper and more credibility-driven for B2B/authority-building content (real estate, finance, coaching, etc.).

## Quality bar — check before delivering

- Does the Context Lean make the *right* viewer want to keep watching, without misleading the *wrong* viewer into clicking (no bait-and-switch)?
- Is the Scroll-Stop line short and does it use a genuine contrast word?
- Does the Snapback actually deliver something surprising/valuable — not just a rephrasing of the setup?
- Is every sentence in the hook as short as it can be while staying clear?
- Would this hook still work if read with zero visuals, on pure audio? (If not, it's relying too much on the visual crutch — strengthen the words.)
