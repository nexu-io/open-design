# Documentary Scripting — Long-Form Script Structure

A documentary-style YouTube video (10–30+ minutes) is not a long reel. It follows a
different logic — chapter-based structure, research-heavy body, emotional arc built over
time. The principles overlap with Storyteller's CART framework, but the execution is
specific to long-form video.

---

## Core Principle

A documentary script has two layers working simultaneously:
1. **The information layer** — facts, data, timeline, expert quotes
2. **The narrative layer** — emotion, stakes, conflict, resolution

Most beginner documentary scripts have only layer 1. They feel like Wikipedia articles
read aloud. Layer 2 is what makes Dhruv Rathee's videos feel like films and Saklin Khan's
feel like investigations.

**Blend with Storyteller skill:** Use CART structure to build your documentary arc, and
P.A.S.T. to write the emotional high-points within each chapter. When Storyteller skill
is active, refer to its references for exact templates.

---

## Documentary Script Structure

```
INTRO       → Hook + Stakes + Promise (what will this video reveal)
CHAPTER 1   → WHO + Context (surface facts, orienting the viewer)
CHAPTER 2   → WHAT + WHEN (core issues, timeline, how it escalated)
CHAPTER 3   → WHERE + WHY + HOW (real examples, root causes, mechanisms)
CONCLUSION  → Takeaway + CTA
```

Each chapter follows its own mini-arc: opening beat → conflict → resolution → bridge to
next chapter. Never end a chapter on a flat note — always close with a cliffhanger,
a provocative question, or a statistic that demands the next chapter.

---

## Section 1: Introduction

The intro has one job: make the viewer feel they cannot afford to stop watching.

**Structure:**
```
[Opening hook — scene drop or shocking statement (0–30 sec)]
[Stakes statement — why this topic matters to THIS viewer]
[Preview — what they will discover by the end]
[Chapter bridge — transition into Chapter 1]
```

**Hook options for documentary intros:**
- Drop straight into a scene: "It's 2024. A 22-year-old in Mumbai just lost ₹80,000 on
  Dream11 in a single IPL match. He is not alone."
- Shocking statistic first: "India has 80 million content creators. 95% of them will
  never cross 10,000 subscribers. Today we find out why."
- Provocative question: "What if the thing you think is entertainment is actually
  the most expensive habit of your generation?"

**Stakes statement:**
Tell the viewer explicitly why this concerns THEM. Not the world in general — them
specifically. "If you watch IPL, bet on fantasy cricket, or have ever opened Dream11,
this video is directly about you."

**Promise:**
"By the end of this video, you will understand exactly how this works, why it targets
people exactly like you, and what you can actually do about it."

The promise creates an obligation — the viewer now wants to hold you to it.

---

## Section 2: Chapter 1 — WHO + Context

Chapter 1 orients the viewer in the world of your topic. It is the "Context" (C) from
CART — but expanded to fill 3–5 minutes.

**What to cover:**
- What is this thing? (brief, not exhaustive)
- When and how did it start?
- Who are the main players? (heroes, villains, stakeholders, victims)
- How big is it? (scale data — numbers, revenue, reach)

**Tone:** Informative but building curiosity. You are painting the scene before the
conflict enters. Move quickly — viewers who came for the controversy don't want 10
minutes of background.

**Target length:** 600–800 words in script (3–5 minutes of video)

**Transition into Chapter 2:**
End Chapter 1 with a question or a statement that makes Chapter 2 feel inevitable.
> "But there's something the numbers don't tell you. And that something is exactly
> what we're going to uncover next."

---

## Section 3: Chapter 2 — WHAT + WHEN (Core Issues)

This is where the documentary earns its name. Chapter 2 is the Adversity section from
CART — the conflict, the scandal, the hidden mechanism, the thing viewers came to learn.

**What to cover:**
- The core problem or controversy in full detail
- Timeline: when did this start, how did it escalate?
- Surprising or little-known facts (the data the mainstream won't tell you)
- How the problem affects real people — not abstract populations

**Tone:** Investigative. This is where your voice shifts from "tour guide" to "journalist."
Use language that signals you've done work others haven't:
> "What I found when I dug deeper was something most people don't talk about..."
> "The data tells a very different story..."
> "Here's what actually happens behind the scenes..."

**P.A.S.T. usage (when Storyteller skill active):**
If you have a real-person case study (someone who was affected by your topic), zoom into
their story here using P.A.S.T. — Place, Action, Speech, Thoughts. This is the chapter
where an individual story carries more emotional weight than 10 statistics.

**Target length:** 800–1200 words (5–7 minutes)

**Transition into Chapter 3:**
> "But understanding what happened isn't enough. We need to understand WHY this keeps
> happening — and that's where this story gets truly alarming."

---

## Section 4: Chapter 3 — WHERE + WHY + HOW (Real Examples + Root Causes)

Chapter 3 goes from "what is the problem" to "why does this problem exist and how does
it sustain itself." This is the chapter that makes viewers share the video.

**What to cover:**
- Real geographic or demographic examples (cities, age groups, communities)
- The systemic reasons the problem exists — who benefits from it continuing?
- The mechanisms: how does this actually work step by step?
- Expert voices (quote or show an expert who validates your argument)
- The psychological or economic driver

**Tone:** Analysis. This is where you show you understand the subject deeply. Viewers
who finish Chapter 3 feeling like they understand something new will share the video.

**Manipulation techniques (used ethically):**
Documentary creators use psychological framing to strengthen their arguments. Used honestly,
these are legitimate persuasion tools:

- **Heroic/Villain framing:** Show the entities that benefit from the problem as the
  antagonists. Show the affected people as the protagonists. Be factual — don't lie —
  but choose what you emphasize.
- **Selective expert use:** Feature experts whose statements support your argument.
  You are not obligated to include every side equally — but do not misrepresent
  what experts say.
- **Repetition:** State your core argument 3–4 times across the video in different
  ways. First time, viewers note it. Third time, they believe it. This is how
  effective documentary makers drive a thesis home.
- **Music as emotion:** Dramatic music under a devastating statistic. Silence under a
  powerful quote. These are tools, not tricks — they make information feel like story.

**Target length:** 800–1200 words (5–7 minutes)

---

## Section 5: Conclusion

**Structure:**
```
[Callback to intro hook — close the loop you opened]
[Summary — 2–3 sentences recapping the arc]
[Takeaway — one sentence lesson (from Storyteller's CART T)]
[Call to action — what the viewer should DO now]
[Outro]
```

**Callback example:**
If your intro opened with a 22-year-old losing ₹80,000 on Dream11 — return to him here.
What happened after? Did he learn? Did he quit? The callback gives the video a cinematic
full-circle feel that makes it feel like a film rather than an essay.

**Takeaway:**
One sentence. The same rule as Storyteller's CART. Not three insights — one.
> "The real problem with IPL isn't the cricket — it's a $3 billion industry that has
> been engineered to make you spend, and most people will never notice."

**Call to action:**
Documentary CTAs are different from reel CTAs. Options:
- "If this video made you think differently, share it with someone who needs to see it."
- "Drop in the comments: were you aware of this before today?"
- "Watch [next video link] to understand the next part of this story."

## Script Generation Workflow (AI-Assisted)

After your research document is organized (see research-workflow.md), convert it to a
full script using this method:

**Step 1:** Open ChatGPT or DeepSeek with a new session.

**Step 2:** Paste this system prompt first:
```
I have researched and structured my documentary data. I need you to convert each
section into a full narration script. Rules:
- Do NOT generate sections I haven't provided
- Do NOT summarize — expand with natural narration
- Write in natural Hindi as if I am speaking to camera
- Use smooth transitions between paragraphs
- Add emotional depth, suspense-building questions, real-life examples
- Target: each section I give you should produce 400–600 words of script
- Format: [NARRATOR] for voiceover lines, [CUT TO] for visual cues
When ready, say "Paste your first section."
```

**Step 3:** Paste one section at a time (Intro, then Chapter 1, then Chapter 2, etc.)
in separate messages. Do not paste all at once — the AI will rush and flatten the quality.

**Step 4:** After each section generates, paste it into your master script document.
Do not accept the first output blindly — read it and note where it sounds robotic or
uses AI-typical phrasing. Flag those sentences and rewrite them manually.

**Step 5:** Paste the full script back into the AI and ask:
```
Review this full script and identify:
1. Sentences that sound unnatural or overly formal
2. Sections where the pacing is too slow (too many filler words)
3. Any missing transitions between chapters
Suggest specific rewrites for each issue.
```

**Step 6:** Make the manual revisions. The AI handles structure. You handle voice.
Your personal touch — your specific examples, your actual opinions, your real experiences —
is what makes the final script yours and not a generic AI output.

## Word Count and Length Reference

| Script Length | Word Count | Video Duration |
|---|---|---|
| Short documentary | 1500 words | 8–10 min |
| Standard documentary | 2500–3000 words | 15–20 min |
| Long documentary | 5000+ words | 30+ min |

**Speaking rate reference:** Most Hindi/Hinglish narrators speak at 140–160 words per
minute in documentary style (slower than casual conversation, faster than formal lecture).

Use 150 words per minute as your baseline.

## Common Documentary Script Mistakes

| Mistake | Why it fails | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Starting with "Aaj hum baat karenge..." | Slow, no hook, viewer drops | Start with a scene or shocking stat |
| All data, no story | Feels like a Wikipedia article | Add one human case study per chapter |
| Weak chapter endings | Viewer doesn't feel compelled to continue | End every chapter on a question or alarming beat |
| Too many takeaways in conclusion | Dilutes the message | One takeaway sentence, then CTA |
| No callback to intro | Video feels incomplete | Return to your opening scene in the conclusion |
| Copying source videos too closely | Copyright, credibility, algorithm | Minimum 40% new data or angle from your own research |
